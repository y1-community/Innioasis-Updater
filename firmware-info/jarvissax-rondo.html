Rondo

**A click-wheel firmware for the Innioasis Y1: the music on your card, YouTube Music, YouTube and FM radio, all from the wheel.**

Install · Use · Dashboard · Server · Help · What's new

The Innioasis Y1 is a small music player with an iPod-style click wheel. Underneath, it runs Android 4.2. Rondo is a firmware for it, built from the community's Android 4.2 base with Android's whole app layer taken out: the Y1 starts straight into Rondo, a program of its own that draws every screen itself, 60 times a second, with menus made for the wheel. Play the music on your microSD card, stream YouTube Music and YouTube straight from the Y1, and listen to FM radio, all without a touchscreen. You install it with Innioasis Updater CE, the same way as Rockbox or the Y1's original software, and you can always go back.

What it does

Your music, from the card

Copy music onto the microSD card and Rondo sorts it by song, artist, album artist, album and genre, using the files' tags. You get covers, Cover Flow, lyrics that scroll along with the song, search as you type, ratings, and playlists you make on the Y1 or in your browser, including smart playlists that pick their songs by rules. Library also has the videos and photos on the card, and a file manager. Organize the card puts everything in the folders Jellyfin, Plex and Kodi expect, after showing you every move. Plugging the Y1 into a computer turns the card into a USB drive.

Every common music format: MP3, AAC/M4A, ALAC, FLAC, Ogg, Opus, WAV, AIFF, WMA, APE, WavPack, AC-3, DTS, DSD and more. Lyrics come from the file's tags or an .lrc file next to it, and videos show the subtitle files beside them. Nothing on the card ever needs the internet.

YouTube Music and YouTube

Straight from the Y1 over Wi-Fi, with nothing to set up: home, search, artists, albums, playlists, up next, lyrics, Shorts, and video up to 720p with subtitles. Sign in with a code, the way you'd sign in a smart TV, for your own home, subscriptions, history and playlists, or use it without an account. Download a video to the card to watch it with no connection. If you'd rather the Y1 never talked to YouTube itself, a Rondo server of your own can do that part instead.

Made for the wheel

Every screen works with the wheel and five buttons. Each wheel pulse is one step, like Rockbox, with an optional buzz and click on every step and acceleration for long lists. Screens slide in and out like an iPod's, and lists glide. Searches, Wi-Fi passwords and playlist names are typed on a keyboard you move through with the wheel or the buttons. Choose a light or dark look, an accent color and a text size, and which entries the main menu shows, in which order.

Settings

Sound

An equalizer with presets or ten bands of your own, kept for each output: the speaker, wired headphones and each Bluetooth device. A loudness boost whose peaks never clip, volume leveling, the Y1's amplifier up to 6 dB louder, and a volume limit. With a server, Headphone match corrects the sound for your headphone model.

FM radio, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi

The Y1's FM radio, with station names, a scan of the whole band, saved stations and a speaker option; wired headphones are the antenna. Bluetooth headphones and speakers, and Wi-Fi networks, are set up from the wheel as well. A wired headset's buttons work too.

A dashboard in your browser

Open Settings › Dashboard on the Y1, then the address it shows on your phone or computer. From there you can upload music, videos and photos, manage the files on the card, make playlists and smart playlists, control what's playing, change the theme, the sound and every other setting, manage Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, YouTube and the radio, and shut down, restart or update the Y1. It runs on the Y1 itself, over your Wi-Fi.

Updates that keep everything

A new Rondo installs in about a minute, without reinstalling the firmware: from **Settings › System › Software update over Wi-Fi (straight from GitHub, no server needed), with Install** in the dashboard, or over USB from the update page. Your settings, Wi-Fi, playlists and music stay as they are. The rare release that changes the firmware itself says so, and installs with Innioasis Updater CE instead.

What you need

  • An Innioasis Y1, Type A: one that shipped with Innioasis's own software 2.0.0 or later. Not

    sure which type yours is? See Before you start.

  • A computer (macOS, Windows or Linux) with Innioasis Updater CE, and a USB-C cable that

    carries data, for the install.

  • 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi for YouTube, updates and the dashboard. The Y1 can't see 5 GHz networks.
  • Optional: a Rondo server of your own, on a computer at home that stays on

    (a 64-bit Raspberry Pi or a NAS is fine) or a small VPS. YouTube works without it.

Getting started

  1. Install Rondo with Innioasis Updater CE: choose the Y1, Type A, then

    Rondo. It takes about 10 minutes, and your microSD card isn't touched.

  2. First start: join Wi-Fi, copy your music onto the card, and

    look through Settings. YouTube Music and YouTube work as soon as the Y1 is online.

  3. Sign in to YouTube if you like: Settings › YouTube › YouTube account shows a code to

    enter at google.com/device.

  4. Learn the controls, and open the dashboard on

    your phone.

Your own server (optional)

You don't need a server: Rondo talks to YouTube itself. A Rondo server is for those who'd like one anyway. With it, the Y1 never talks to YouTube or Google: the server browses YouTube for it, finds the streams with yt-dlp (which keeps up with YouTube's changes and updates itself every day) and passes them on. It has its own YouTube account, signed in on its admin page, and it adds Headphone match. You run it with Docker at home or on a VPS, set it up from its admin page in the browser and connect your Y1 with a six-digit code; a new version installs with one click there. One server serves one Y1. See Setting up a Rondo server.

Rondo has its own YouTube client built in: it browses YouTube Music and YouTube the way their apps do and plays the streams as they arrive. With **Settings › YouTube › Connection › My server**, the Y1 talks only to your server instead, which browses YouTube with youtubei.js, finds the streams with yt-dlp and passes audio and video through to the Y1 in a form it can play.

The project

FolderWhat's in it
native/Rondo itself: the program the firmware runs (C++ with the NDK, its own GLES2 UI, FFmpeg), with its YouTube client in native/direct/ and the dashboard's page in native/assets/dashboard/
firmware/The script that builds the firmware (rom.zip) from the community's base ROM, and Rondo's start script
server/The optional Rondo server (Node 22, TypeScript, Fastify)
infra/Docker and Caddy files for running the server
web-updater/The update page (WebUSB), published on GitHub Pages
tools/The native build and bundle scripts, mock servers for UI work, the boot screen generator, the version script
docs/Guides, plus notes on the Y1's internals and API references

Every release is built the same way: the signed Rondo bundle goes up, and GitHub Actions builds the firmware from it and publishes the update page. To build it yourself, start with Building Rondo.

Thanks

Rondo stands on other people's work:

  • The y1-community: its

    Innioasis Updater CE, which installs Rondo, and its Android 4.2 base ROM, with the Y1's Wi-Fi switched on, which Rondo's firmware is built from.

  • Rockbox's Y1 port, whose wheel handling Rondo

    copies, and the JJ Launcher and Inniclassic launchers, which showed how the Y1's buttons behave.

  • On the Y1: FFmpeg, curl, OpenSSL, FreeType, HarfBuzz, SQLite,

    libpng, libjpeg-turbo, yyjson, nghttp2, and the Roboto and **Noto Color Emoji fonts. On the server: youtubei.js, yt-dlp, Fastify and sharp**. AutoEq's measurements are behind Headphone match, and LRCLIB has many of the lyrics. See THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.
